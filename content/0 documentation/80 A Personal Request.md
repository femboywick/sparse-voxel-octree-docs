## What Is AI?
A terribly defined word. 
> The capability of computer systems or algorithms to imitate intelligent human behaviour[^webster]

[^webster]: This was the Merriam Webster definition as of 2026: https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/artificial%20intelligence

This is an umbrella term. For a vast category at that. This includes many things, such as:
- A computer imitating a human's inputs into a game, like how [SethBling trained a machine-learning model to play Mario](https://youtu.be/qv6UVOQ0F44). This is somewhat related to game AI, where a game designer desires enemy or non-player movement and actions to be executed. Ironically, this library's final product (the path-finding) is a form of AI in itself.
- The AI behind the oppression of the human race in The Matrix. A fictional example, but one that's prevalent in pop culture. (or was, at least...)
- Finally, this also includes the types of AI that you are most used to seeing nowadays. Generative AI.

## By Humans, for Humans
[Daniele Procida](https://vurt.eu) wrote [a wonderful article](https://vurt.eu/articles/my-favourite-german-word/), which starts with the premise "whats the point of writing good, user-oriented documentation, when the user is likely to feed it into an AI anyway?". Over the course of the article, he introduces his favourite German word, and takes the reader on a long journey from philosophy back to documentation. The conclusion was, *write for humans anyway*.

This documentation, my code, and my work in general is designed *for humans*. AI will never be a concern of mine.

The whole point of this project was to parse through some academic research, understand it enough to apply it, and then document it so that it is usable by anyone. A lot of academic research is notoriously verbose. It also tends to assume *a lot* of reader knowledge. Lucky for you, dear reader, I've done some of the work for you. I've produced a solution, and I've documented it thoroughly. 

You could feed my documentation and code into your favourite code-oriented LLM, and ask it to translate it to JavaScript. And hey, there's a chance that maybe it'll do a half-okay job.
